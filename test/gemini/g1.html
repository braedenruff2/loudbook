Chapter One: The Lantern Road

The road out of Harrow Vale was older than the village it served. Wren had walked it every morning for six years, and she knew every stone that wobbled under a careless foot.

Today the stones were wet, and the fog lay so thick on the fields that the hedges looked like walls. She carried a lantern even though the sun was up. Her grandmother had told her never to trust a morning fog.

“Things walk in it,” the old woman had said. “Things that should have stayed asleep.”

Wren had laughed at the time. She was not laughing now. Somewhere ahead of her, a bell was ringing. It was slow and heavy, like a bell at a funeral.

There was no church on the lantern road. There never had been. She stopped at the old milestone and held the lantern higher.

The light did not reach far. It made a small gold room in the grey, and beyond its edges the fog moved. “Hello?” she called. Her voice sounded thin and far too young.

The bell stopped. For a long moment there was nothing at all, not even the sound of the brook beside the road.

Then a man stepped out of the fog. He was tall and thin, and he wore a coat the colour of wet ash. “You should not be out here,” he said. “Not today.”

“I walk this road every day,” said Wren. “I have never seen you on it.”

“That is because I have never needed to be seen.”

He looked at the lantern, and something in his face changed. “Where did you get that?”

“It was my grandmother's. She gave it to me before she died.”

The man was quiet for a while. When he spoke again, his voice was softer. “Then you had better keep it lit. Whatever happens, do not let it go out.”

Wren wanted to ask him why, but he had already turned away. She watched him walk into the fog until there was nothing left of him but the sound of his boots.

Then even that was gone.
