Chapter Two: The Mill at the Ford

The mill stood where the brook met the river, and it had not turned in twenty years. Wren reached it a little after noon. The fog had thinned by then, but it had not lifted.

The great wheel was black with moss, and the windows were dark. She knocked on the door anyway, because that was what her grandmother would have done.

Nobody answered. The door swung open on its own, slow and silent. Inside, the air smelled of flour and cold iron.

A fire was burning in the hearth, which made no sense at all. “Is anyone here?” she asked.

“Only me,” said a voice from the stairs.

A girl came down, about her own age, with flour on her hands and a knife in her belt. “My name is Tamsin,” she said. “You're the one with the lantern.”

“How do you know about the lantern?”

“Everyone in the valley knows about the lantern. Everyone except you, it seems.”

Tamsin sat down by the fire and pointed to the chair across from her. Wren sat. The lantern hissed quietly at her feet.

“Your grandmother kept the road,” said Tamsin. “Every night for fifty years, she walked it with that light.”

“Why?”

“To keep the sleepers sleeping. And now she's gone, and the bell is ringing again.”

Wren thought of the tall man in the grey coat, and of how he had looked at the lantern. “I think I met one of them,” she said.

Tamsin's face went pale. “Then we have less time than I thought.” Outside, far away, the bell began to ring.
